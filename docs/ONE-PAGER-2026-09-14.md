# quantum-cortex — one page for the industry (2026-09-14)

**What this is.** A memory organ for language models that can be measured, a benchmark that measures it, and a method that lets one person and an AI system produce results without fooling themselves. Built from scratch in Brussels by Win2Win SRL, in the open, with every number tied to an artefact and every failure kept on the record.

**The question it answers.** Does a system keep what happened to it? Not "does it remember on average" but the dissociation Henry Molaison taught neurology in 1953: cut the memory organ and the episodes must vanish while the skills stay. We froze that question into a protocol with its thresholds before any result existed, and we run it on everything we build.

## Three things you can use today

**1. The benchmark: the Molaison dissociation, open and frozen.** A specification (`docs/benchmarks/hm-protocol.md`, thresholds frozen 2026-08-08), a reference runner, and results you can reproduce on a laptop in minutes (`notebooks/reproduce_molaison_cpu.ipynb`). It has passed on our memory organ on four seeds with the journal sealed on disk and reopened by a new process, and it has failed six times, with attribution, on our 27M language model. A benchmark that knows how to fail is one you can trust with your own model.

**2. The organ: a sealed, gated, restart proof episodic memory.** A write path that admits only what is new, a journal sealed at rest with one key per scope, a read path that returns records with their pointers, a lifecycle that consolidates and evicts on a rhythm, and a contract: cite a stored record or abstain. On a fictional legal matter followed from January to May, a fresh process months later cites the right record for every question it was asked about and abstains on every question that was never written down (`cortex_c2b/companion_demo.py`, transcript in `metrics/mqar/companion-demo-2026-09-14.json`). Today the organ is decoded by a contract or by a larger model; teaching a small model to read it in generation is the research edge (see below).

**3. The method: an evidentiary discipline you can copy.** Seven admission rules before a number becomes a claim; one variable per run; thresholds that never move after a run; every log retained as a file before any number is read; a ledger with one record per run and its configuration hash; failures attributed to a component and kept. Six fine tunes on the model arm all failed the gate; each said why; the seventh tests a mechanism declared before its run. That is what "reproducible" means here.

## What we do not claim

- No general capability. Where continuity is not decisive, use a frontier model.
- The 27M model does not yet read its own journal reliably in generation: it retrieves the right episode (98 %) and reads it (98 % of its attention), and the decision to cite or abstain is the measured limit of six data recipes. A closing test decides this week whether a mechanism passes at this size; if not, the limit is written and carried to a larger run.
- The oracle channel (a model whose answers change on the day the world changes) is not built. The device sized encrypted model is not built. The hybrids of recurrent memories showed no memory advantage at this size; that negative result is on the record too.

## Who it is for

- Teams shipping agents that must remember decisions across months: the organ, with your model as the reader.
- Laboratories publishing long memory models: the benchmark, with its frozen thresholds, on your own weights.
- Anyone building with an AI collaborator who wants a method that survives scrutiny: the repository is the method.

## Try it

`git clone` the repository, `pytest tests/ -m "not slow"` (about a minute), then `python -m cortex_c2b.hm_protocol --persistent --seed 0` for the dissociation and `python -m cortex_c2b.companion_demo` for the companion. Every claim above has a row in `docs/RESULTS.md` with its artefact and its test.
