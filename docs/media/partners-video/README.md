# The presentation for partners: how the video is made

*The source of `quantum-cortex-partners-v1.1.0.mp4`, the presentation video for partners linked from the first screen of the README, kept so that the video can be made again, scene by scene, from text (2026-11-11). The README's poster, `docs/figures/partners-video-poster.jpg`, opens the 1280 by 720 copy committed beside this folder, `docs/media/quantum-cortex-partners-v1.1.0-720p.mp4` (8 706 871 bytes; sha256 `3b1c4eb91e95778c12e9453ebaaebc2acb4f572b04e326ed958d90bdd0ddd3f2`; 215 s, 30 frames per second, re-encoded from the finished file); the 1920 by 1080 file, `quantum-cortex-partners-v1.1.0.mp4` (31 322 148 bytes; sha256 `a8ec56dbddf61982f408d14decda11337276885e91e08f39ef182209b50d1685`), stays with the author and is not committed (register, Rev138). Every statement of fact on screen is the repository's text or its close paraphrase, read sentence by sentence by a committee; the hook and the author's assumptions are labelled on screen as the author's and not measured, the eviction beat as an illustration; the tagline, the scene titles and the call to partners are the author's framing. The video carries no measured value of the record (register, Rev136 and Rev138; the committee's reading in the workshop's working notes). Licences: the six source files of this folder are code, under the Apache License 2.0 (`LICENSE`, `NOTICE`; `LICENSE-DOCS.md`, its scope paragraph and its dated line of 2026-11-11); this README, the poster and the video are documentation under CC BY 4.0 (`LICENSE-DOCS.md`).*

- `page.html`: one web page, 1920 by 1080, holding the thirteen scenes and one timeline. `window.seek(t)` draws the page at second `t`: the globe of nodes and arcs on a canvas, every element's opacity and position from its `data-in` and `data-out` times, the typed questions, the score bars, the travelling dots of the read path, the roadmap's progress line. Nothing is animated by the browser's clock, so a frame is the same wherever it is drawn.
- `render.js`: draws one scene at a time at 30 frames per second with a headless browser (Node, Playwright with its Chromium), screenshots each frame as a JPEG and pipes them into `ffmpeg` (`libx264`, `crf 18`, `yuv420p`), one encoded file per scene; `scenes.json` beside them lists the scenes and their times.
- `concat.sh`: joins the scene files with the concat demuxer and no second encoding.
- `music.py`: the score, synthesised from scratch with `numpy` and `scipy` (no sample, no existing work): a slow bed in D minor at 84 beats per minute, built on the scene times of `scenes.json`, kept under the picture (minus 24.2 LUFS integrated, a sample peak of minus 14.24 dBFS, measured on the finished file with the `ebur128` and `astats` filters of `ffmpeg`; the first mix sat 6 dB higher and was lowered at the author's request). The full file is made with `ffmpeg -i <silent.mp4> -i <music.wav> -map 0:v -map 1:a -c:v copy -c:a aac -b:a 192k -shortest -movflags +faststart <out.mp4>` (the index first, so that a browser starts before the whole file is fetched). The 720p copy: the same inputs with `-vf scale=1280:720:flags=lanczos -c:v libx264 -crf 23 -preset slow -pix_fmt yuv420p -c:a aac -b:a 96k`.
- `preview.js`: draws a few frames at full size at given times, to look at them before any full render. `script.js`: extracts every on screen text with its scene and its time (the script the committee read).

Fonts: Inter (the text) and DejaVu Sans Mono (the console); no font is embedded: a frame is the same wherever it is drawn with the same fonts installed; with another sans serif face the lines wrap differently (Helvetica or Arial and any monospace face render the page). The programs' outputs shown in the console scene (s3) and in the try it scene (s4) are the real outputs of `python -m cortex_c2b.companion_demo` and `python -m cortex_c2b.hm_protocol --persistent`, run on the tree of `4e988c5`, whose code the tag's tree carries unchanged (zip 64 changed no code); the eviction beat is labelled on screen as an illustration, not the program's output.
